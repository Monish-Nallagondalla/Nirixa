## 🔬 Pillar 1: Scientific Methodology Mastery

Academic review panels prioritize candidates with methodological precision. Candidates should master:

1. **Validated Psychometric Instruments**:
   * **NASA-TLX (Task Load Index)**: Quantifies mental demand, physical demand, temporal demand, performance, effort, and frustration.
   * **SUS (System Usability Scale)** & **UMUX-Lite**: Standardized usability quantification.
   * **Cognitive Offloading Probes**: Measuring reaction latency and error detection rates under AI copilot assistance.
2. **Statistical Analysis Stack (Python / R)**:
   * Master `pingouin` and `statsmodels` for:
     * Mixed-Effects Linear Models (for longitudinal repeated-measures).
     * Two-Way Repeated Measures ANOVA.
     * Effect Sizes (Cohen’s $d$, Partial $\eta^2$).
     * Statistical Power Calculations ($N$, $\alpha=0.05$, $\beta=0.80$).

---

## 🛠️ Pillar 2: Open Source System as an Instrumented Research Apparatus

Open-source systems (such as Nirixa) serve as reproducible research laboratories:

* **Longitudinal Telemetry**: Logging interaction patterns, prompt revisions, and task execution times.
* **Empirical Cohorts**: Running controlled ablation studies (Human Only vs. Human + Stateless Copilot vs. Human + Stateful Agent).
* **Research Advantage**: Presenting a functioning, deployed platform with telemetry significantly outranks purely theoretical proposals.

---

## 📄 Pillar 3: Pre-PhD Preprint & Workshop Track

Having one peer-reviewed workshop paper or polished arXiv preprint establishes immediate research credibility.
* Synthesize core findings into a short empirical paper (e.g., CHI, CSCW, or IUI workshop).
* Focus on empirical measurements rather than speculative claims.

---

## 🧭 Pillar 4: Targeted Lab Outreach

European PhD positions are salaried research roles ($€50\text{k}-€75\text{k}$/year). Contact professors with specific research alignment:
* Review 2-3 of the lab's most recent publications.
* Propose concrete extensions of their work using your instrumented platform.
