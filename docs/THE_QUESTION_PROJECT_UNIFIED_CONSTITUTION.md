## II. The Five Pillars of Intelligence

1. **Questions**: What is a question? Why do questions exist? Can questions be represented computationally? Are questions more fundamental than answers?
2. **Intelligence**: Human, artificial, collective, and goal-directed intelligence.
3. **Memory**: Storage, associative retrieval, reflection, compression, forgetting, and lived experience.
4. **Identity**: *Who am I? Who should I become? How does continuous questioning reveal and construct identity?*
5. **Evolution**: Preserving the trajectory of how thoughts, questions, and personal paradigms change over decades.

## III. Personal Research Questions

- **Intelligence**: How do humans collect information? Why do humans notice different things? Why do identical experiences affect people differently?
- **AI**: Can AI have authentic experiences? Can AI formulate intrinsic goals? Can AI become curious? Can AI maintain unresolved open loops across years?

## V. The 10 Frontier Research Areas (The Open Inquiries)

1. **Attention**: How is attention allocated internally? Can artificial attention ever model human salience?
2. **Emotion**: Does emotion act as the heuristic weight that guides memory retrieval and prioritizes thoughts?
3. **Surprise**: Humans learn most profoundly when prediction fails (surprise). Can AI learn from epistemic surprise?
4. **Motivation**: Where does sustained human drive originate when rewards are distant or non-existent?
5. **Intuition**: Can subconscious heuristic pattern-matching be modeled computationally?
6. **Taste**: Why do individuals develop vastly different aesthetics, standards, and discernment?
7. **Wisdom**: How does wisdom differ from raw processing intelligence or knowledge?
8. **Creativity**: How do genuinely novel conceptual leaps emerge beyond latent space interpolation?
9. **Meaning**: How do biological organisms assign semantic and existential meaning to events?
10. **Values**: Where do human ethical and architectural values originate?
